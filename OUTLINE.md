# Handbook outline

The chapter list, the required content of each chapter, and the roadmap projects each chapter serves. Authors follow `AUTHORING.md` for form and this file for substance. Required items are requirements, not suggestions; add what is needed for completeness, remove nothing.

## Part I: Foundations (Phase 0)

### Chapter 1: Tokenization and Data Representation
Serves P0.2, P1.1. Required: why models see integers, not text; byte-level BPE algorithm step by step with a worked merge example on a tiny corpus; byte fallback and why no token is ever unknown; pre-tokenization rules (whitespace, digits, code) and their consequences; vocabulary size trade-offs (embedding table size, bytes per token, rare-token learning) with numbers for 16k, 32k, 50k, 128k vocabularies; bytes per token as a property of tokenizer and data (English versus Hindi versus code); special tokens (BOS, EOS, padding, chat-role markers) and how they are reserved and trained; chat templates as a token-layout contract, with one real template structure shown abstractly; packing with EOS separators and memory-mapped shards; label shifting by one; how tokenization errors show up in training (fluent loss, garbage samples). Listings: a minimal BPE trainer (merge loop) and an encode function; a packing routine that writes uint16 shards. Diagrams: pipeline text to ids; the merge process; packing layout. Exercises: compute bytes per token from a sample; explain why a chat-template mismatch silently degrades quality; derive embedding table size for given vocab and width.

### Chapter 2: The Transformer, Derived
Serves P0.2, P1.1, Chapter 4. Required: the residual stream as the organizing idea; token embeddings; position: learned absolute versus RoPE, with the RoPE rotation written out for a 2-d pair, the relative-position property derived (the dot product of rotated q and k depends only on position difference), and why this generalizes better; attention from scratch: Q, K, V projections, scores, the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor derived from the variance of a dot product of independent unit-variance components, causal masking with negative infinity before softmax, weighted sum of V, output projection; multi-head attention and what heads buy; grouped-query and multi-query attention with the KV-cache motivation and the parameter and cache arithmetic for Llama-3-8B (32 query heads, 8 KV heads, head dim 128); the MLP block, GELU versus SwiGLU with the gating written out and the parameter count of a SwiGLU block (three matrices); LayerNorm versus RMSNorm formulas, pre-norm versus post-norm and training stability; weight tying; the LM head and logits; parameter counting: derive $\approx 12 L d^2$ for non-embedding parameters (attention $4d^2$, MLP $8d^2$ for a 4x expansion), then the SwiGLU variant, plus embeddings $V d$; worked count for GPT-2 small and for Qwen2.5-1.5B shapes (state assumptions); FlashAttention in one section: what it changes (IO-aware tiling, no materialized attention matrix) and why it matters for memory, not FLOPs; the KV cache mechanism at inference and why decode is sequential. Listings: RoPE application; a causal self-attention module with GQA; a full Llama-style block; a tiny model class assembling N blocks (under 60 lines each, PyTorch). Diagrams: block diagram; attention data flow; GQA head sharing; residual stream view. Exercises: derive the scaling factor; count parameters for a given config; compute KV-cache bytes per token; explain why a broken causal mask drives loss to near zero.

### Chapter 3: Training Dynamics
Serves P0.2, P1.1, P1.2. Required: cross-entropy loss and its value at initialization ($\ln V$), perplexity; initialization scale and why residual branches are scaled down in deep stacks; AdamW update equations with bias correction, what the two moments are, weight decay as a separate term, typical betas for pretraining versus fine-tuning; learning-rate schedules: linear warmup, cosine decay, the warmup-stable-decay alternative, why warmup is needed (empty second-moment estimates, large early steps); gradient clipping by global norm; gradient accumulation and effective batch, and why the learning rate should be set for the effective batch; tokens-per-step as the right batch unit; mixed precision: fp32, fp16, bf16 bit layouts, dynamic range, loss scaling algorithm for fp16 (scale, backward, unscale, skip on inf), why bf16 needs none, master weights in fp32; what a checkpoint must contain for exact resume (model, optimizer, scheduler, step, data position, RNG); data loading as a bottleneck and how to detect it (GPU utilization); reading loss curves with a table of pathologies (leakage, LR too high, dead LR, overfitting, throttling); overfit-one-batch as a correctness test; validation protocol (fixed subset, periodic sampling). Listings: AdamW step written out in PyTorch tensors (educational); the training loop with accumulation, clipping, autocast, and GradScaler; the checkpoint save and load pair. Diagrams: the loop; the fp16 loss-scaling decision flow; the schedule shape described (as a flowchart of phases). Exercises: compute initial loss for V=16k; compute effective batch; explain a specific NaN scenario; design a resume test.

### Chapter 4: Memory, Compute, and Throughput
Serves every training and serving project. Required: bytes per parameter by scenario with the derivation of 16 bytes for mixed-precision AdamW full fine-tuning (bf16 weights 2, bf16 grads 2, fp32 master 4, two fp32 moments 8) and the reduced versions (8-bit Adam, paged optimizers); activation memory scaling with batch, sequence, layers, and width, the order-of-magnitude formula and why long sequences dominate; activation (gradient) checkpointing: what is stored, recompute cost of about a third more compute; the KV-cache formula $2 \cdot L \cdot n_{kv} \cdot d_{head} \cdot \text{bytes}$ per token with worked numbers for Llama-3-8B (about 128 KB per token in bf16) and for a 1.5B model, then per sequence and per 64 concurrent users; LoRA and QLoRA memory arithmetic for 1.5B, 3B, 7B on 8 GB with sequence-length dependence; training FLOPs $\approx 6ND$ and inference FLOPs $\approx 2N$ per token, derivation sketch; MFU definition and realistic ranges (small models memory-bound, 15 to 30 percent; large well-tuned runs 40 to 55 percent); GPU spec sheet reading: peak bf16 tensor TFLOPS, memory bandwidth, and why bandwidth sets decode speed; a roofline explanation with arithmetic intensity, prefill compute-bound versus decode memory-bound; worked run-time estimates: 125M model on 300M tokens on the 4060, on a T4, on an A100; decode tokens per second upper bound from bandwidth divided by bytes read per token, worked for a 7B 4-bit model on the 4060. Listings: a parameter-counting function; a memory-estimate function; a quick benchmark harness measuring tokens per second and peak memory. Diagrams: memory pie for full fine-tuning; roofline as a flowchart of regimes; the "what fits in 8 GB" decision. Exercises: many calculations.

### Chapter 8: Preference Optimization and Reinforcement Learning
Serves P1.3. Required: why SFT is not enough (only positives, no exposure to own errors); the RLHF pipeline: preference data, Bradley-Terry model $P(y_w \succ y_l) = \sigma(r(y_w) - r(y_l))$, reward model loss, the KL-regularized objective $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[r] - \beta \, \mathrm{KL}(\pi \| \pi_{ref})$, its closed-form optimum $\pi^*(y|x) \propto \pi_{ref}(y|x) \exp(r(x,y)/\beta)$, PPO in outline (clipped objective, value network, GAE) and why it is heavy; DPO derived: solve the optimum for $r$, substitute into Bradley-Terry, obtain the DPO loss $-\log \sigma\left(\beta \log\frac{\pi(y_w)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w)} - \beta \log\frac{\pi(y_l)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l)}\right)$, the implicit reward, the role of beta, gradient intuition; ORPO, SimPO, KTO, IPO in one paragraph each with what they drop and when they help; building preference data (on-policy sampling, verifiable scoring, judge tie-breaks, discarding uninformative prompts); RL with verifiable rewards; GRPO: sample a group of G responses, rewards $r_i$, advantage $\hat{A}_i = (r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r))/\mathrm{std}(r)$, the clipped policy-gradient objective with a KL penalty to the reference, no value network, memory implications; reward design for text-to-SQL (correctness, partial credit, format, length, timeouts) and the reward-hacking catalog with fixes; reading RL curves (reward, KL, length, entropy); comparison table SFT versus DPO versus GRPO with when to use which; compute and memory for DPO and GRPO under PEFT on 8 GB. Listings: DPO loss in PyTorch; GRPO advantage and loss sketch; a reward function for SQL execution with timeout. Diagrams: RLHF pipeline; DPO data flow; GRPO loop; reward-hack feedback. Exercises: derive DPO from the optimum; compute advantages for a sample group; explain what happens when all rewards in a group are equal; design a reward to block a named hack.
